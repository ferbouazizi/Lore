Peter Parker is the original and most recognizable Spider-Man in Marvel Comics. Created by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko, he first appeared in Amazing Fantasy #15 in 1962 and has become one of the most iconic superheroes in popular culture.

Peter grew up in Queens, New York, where he was raised by his Aunt May and Uncle Ben after the death of his parents. As a teenager, Peter was an exceptionally intelligent student with a passion for science and engineering, though he often struggled to fit in socially.

His life changed after being bitten by a radioactive spider during a science demonstration. The bite granted him superhuman strength, incredible agility, enhanced reflexes, the ability to cling to walls, and a heightened danger sense known as Spider-Sense. Using his scientific knowledge, Peter also invented mechanical web-shooters that allow him to swing through New York City and capture criminals.

At first, Peter used his new abilities for personal gain. However, after allowing a criminal to escape, that same criminal later murdered Uncle Ben. This tragedy taught Peter the lesson that would guide the rest of his life:

"With great power comes great responsibility."

From that moment onward, Peter dedicated himself to protecting others as Spider-Man. Unlike many superheroes, Peter constantly struggles to balance his responsibilities as both a hero and an ordinary person. He juggles school, work, friendships, financial difficulties, and family while keeping his identity secret.

Peter has worked as a freelance photographer for the Daily Bugle, where newspaper editor J. Jonah Jameson frequently criticizes Spider-Man despite the hero's efforts to protect the city.

Throughout his adventures, Peter forms close relationships with people such as Aunt May, Mary Jane Watson, Gwen Stacy, Harry Osborn, and later Miles Morales, whom he mentors in several stories. He is also an important member of teams including the Avengers and the Fantastic Four on various occasions.

Spider-Man has one of the largest collections of villains in comic book history. His most famous enemies include Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, Venom, Sandman, Electro, Mysterio, Vulture, Lizard, Kraven the Hunter, Kingpin, Scorpion, Rhino, and Carnage.

Although Peter possesses extraordinary powers, his greatest strength is his character. He is compassionate, intelligent, selfless, and determined to help others, even when doing so comes at great personal cost. His stories often explore themes of responsibility, sacrifice, perseverance, and hope.

Across different comic series, films, television shows, and alternate universes, Peter Parker remains the symbol of the classic Spider-Man—a hero who believes that doing the right thing is worth the sacrifices it requires.
